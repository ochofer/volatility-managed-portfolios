Volatility-managed portfolios: the results

Moreira and Muir's (2017) volatility-managed factors, each month's position scaled by the inverse of last month's realised variance, and the real-time critique of Cederburg et al. (2020), replicated on Kenneth R. French's six equity factors. Then the study's own question: what the signal did in the 136 months after Moreira and Muir's sample, May 2015 to August 2026. Every figure is a simulation drawn from the tables committed with the code.

The 136 months after the sample cannot tell any of these differences from zero, and the sign of a Sharpe ratio difference on one sample says nothing about another.

  • After the sample, May 2015 to August 2026, no factor's real-time combination has a Sharpe ratio above the real-time original's by more than its interval allows. E5 is met: the median difference is +0.007, annualised, and the median half-width 0.213. These 136 months cannot tell the differences from zero. The market's volatility-managed Sharpe ratio is 0.55 against the original's 0.78, a difference of −0.23 with an interval of −1.12 to 0.66 (Figure 1).
  • The alpha and the Sharpe ratio answer different questions: the alpha tests what the volatility-managed factor adds to a holding of the original, and the Sharpe ratio difference tests it in the original's place. On Moreira and Muir's sample Ledoit and Wolf's test finds the volatility-managed Sharpe ratio above the original's for momentum alone, a difference of +0.51, annualised, with an interval of 0.07 to 0.96, while the alpha's Newey-West t-statistic is above 1.96 for the market, momentum and RMW (Figure 2). E4 is not met: the two agree in sign and significance for 2 of 6 factors where 5 were expected, though the test rejects 3.7 per cent of 1,000 simulated pairs with equal Sharpe ratios, inside 3.5 to 6.5.
  • The market's position changes by 0.75 of the factor a month on average, and its alpha net of 10 basis points per unit of that change is 4.03 per cent a year with a t-statistic of 2.31, and 3.13 with a t-statistic of 1.81 at 20 (Figure 3). The position's median is 0.94 and its 99th percentile 7.27, against Moreira and Muir's 0.93 and 6.39 (Figure 4). E6 is met, 17 of 17.
  • On each paper's own sample the replication matches Moreira and Muir's Table I for 6 of 6 factors (E1, the market's alpha 4.93 per cent a year with a White standard error of 1.57, against 4.86 and 1.56) and Barroso and Santa-Clara's scaled momentum in 4 of 4 comparisons (E3, a Sharpe ratio of 1.00 against 0.97 and a worst fall of 43.8 per cent against 45.2). Cederburg et al.'s results reproduce in 22 of 24 comparisons, and E2 is not met: the two misses are RMW's, whose history French has revised since Cederburg et al. downloaded it, its mean on their months now 3.12 per cent a year against their 2.92 (Table 8).

What the signal did in the 136 months after Moreira and Muir's sample, with the volatility-managed factor's scale fixed on the data to April 2015 and the real-time combination carried forward.

The study's own question is what the signal did after Moreira and Muir's sample ended. Both constructions run unchanged on May 2015 to August 2026: the volatility-managed factor's scale, c, is fixed on the months to April 2015, and the real-time combination uses only the months before each decision, so no parameter of either was set on these months. Figure 1 shows each difference with its interval. The intervals are wide because the months are 136: the half-widths of the volatility-managed comparisons, annualised, are 0.53 to 0.89 on these months and 0.21 to 0.44 on the 1,065 months of Moreira and Muir's sample (Figure 2). These months therefore cannot tell the differences from zero.

What the volatility-managed factor adds to the original on Moreira and Muir's sample, how the Sharpe ratio test reads it, and what trading it costs.

The test of a Sharpe ratio difference is Ledoit and Wolf's (2008) studentised circular block bootstrap. Before it touches a factor it is run on 1,000 simulated pairs of 1,065 months whose Sharpe ratios are equal by construction, and it rejects 37 of them at the 5 per cent level, 3.7 per cent, inside the range of 3.5 to 6.5 fixed before the run.

The data, the constructions, what is known at each date, the costs, and what the study cannot show.

Method

Data and period. Kenneth R. French's daily and monthly files of the six factors and of the ten portfolios sorted on prior returns, vintage 202609, downloaded at run time and never committed.4 The market, SMB and HML start in July 1926, momentum in January 1927, RMW and CMA in July 1963, and every series is cut at August 2026. Each paper's results are compared on its own months: Moreira and Muir's to April 2015,1 Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang and Yan's to December 2016,2 Barroso and Santa-Clara's from May 1927 to December 2011.3

The signal and the constructions. Each construction uses its paper's own realised variance. The volatility-managed factor holds c over last month's realised variance. The real-time combination holds, each month, the mean-variance weights of an investor with a risk aversion of 5 on the volatility-managed and the original factor, estimated from all the months before it once 120 are available, its total position capped at 5. Scaled momentum holds WML at 12 per cent a year over its realised volatility of the previous 126 trading days. Rebalancing is monthly throughout.

What is known at each date. Each month's position uses only the realised variance of the month before. c is the exception: Moreira and Muir set it on the whole sample, which no investor could have known, and the replication does the same, as the paper does. The real-time combination uses only the months before each decision, and for the months after the sample c is fixed on the months to April 2015, so nothing in those 136 months was set on them.

The test. Ledoit and Wolf (2008) take the standard error of a Sharpe ratio difference by the delta method from the prewhitened Parzen kernel, and its interval from a circular block bootstrap of 2,000 samples of 12-month blocks, each sample's difference studentised by its own standard error.5 The paper proposes the QS kernel and reports the Parzen kernel's performance as very similar, and it calibrates the block length from the data, where the study fixed it at 12 months before the run.

Costs. A month's turnover is the absolute change in the position from the month before. The cost, 10 basis points per unit of turnover and 20 as the sensitivity, is subtracted from the volatility-managed return, and the alpha is estimated again on the net return. The factor's own rebalancing is the same for the managed and the original factor and is left out.

What the study cannot show. It holds no view on expected returns and estimates no premium. Its costs are charged on the change in a factor's position, and a book that holds the stocks pays more, so the break-even costs bound what it could pay. French revises history, so a run on a later vintage need not reproduce every digit, as RMW shows. The 136 months after the sample are too few to tell any Sharpe ratio difference from zero: halving the market's half-width would require four times as many. The volatility-managed position is never capped, as in the paper, so its largest positions, up to 16.8, are positions no mandate would allow.

Terms

Factor
the monthly return of a long-short portfolio that captures one source of common movement in stock returns, as Kenneth French publishes it.
Excess return
a return minus the one-month Treasury bill rate. Every factor is one.
Realised variance
the variance of a factor computed from its daily returns within one month, each paper with its own definition.
The signal
last month's realised variance of a factor, and nothing else.
Volatility-managed
Moreira and Muir's construction, the factor's return times c over last month's realised variance. Managed describes the construction and says nothing about its performance.
c
the constant that gives the volatility-managed factor the original's standard deviation over the whole sample, so it is known only at the sample's end.
Position
the multiple of the factor held in a month. The papers call a position above 1 leverage.
Real-time combination (S1)
Cederburg et al.'s construction, the mean-variance weights on the volatility-managed and the original factor with a risk aversion of 5, estimated each month from the months before it, its total position capped at 5.
Real-time original (S2)
the original factor alone at its real-time mean-variance weight.
Ex post optimal combination (S3)
the mix of the two with the weights that were best over the whole sample, which no investor could have known.
Scaled momentum
Barroso and Santa-Clara's construction, momentum (WML, winners minus losers) times 12 per cent a year over its realised volatility of the previous 126 trading days.
Alpha
the intercept of the regression of the volatility-managed factor on its original, in per cent a year, always with its standard error or t-statistic.
Standard error
the standard deviation of an estimate across the samples it could have come from. White's allows the residual's variance to change, and Newey and West's at 12 lags also allows a month's residual to be correlated with the 12 before it.
Sharpe ratio
the mean excess return over its standard deviation, annualised.
Jobson-Korkie test
Cederburg et al.'s test that two return series over the same months have the same Sharpe ratio, which assumes normal and independent months.
Delta method
the standard error of a function of means, from the function's slopes and the means' covariance.
Appraisal ratio
the alpha over the residual's standard deviation, annualised.
Interval
the 95 per cent interval of Ledoit and Wolf's test for the true Sharpe ratio difference. The test rejects when it excludes zero.
Half-width
the distance from a difference to either end of its interval.
p-value
the probability of a difference at least as far from zero as the one computed, if the two true Sharpe ratios were equal.
Size
the share of samples in which a test rejects when the null is true.
Turnover
the absolute change in the position from the month before, the first month carrying none.
Basis point
a hundredth of a percentage point.
Break-even cost
the cost per unit of turnover at which the alpha falls to zero.
Maximum drawdown
the largest fall from a peak of the compounded return.
Band
the distance from a published number within which a computed one counts as matching, fixed before the code.
Verdict
whether an expectation is met. A count reported in no verdict is shown without one.
Vintage
the version of French's files, named by the CRSP release they were built from.

Optimal versus naive diversification: a replication, and the same question under an index mandate. Replicates DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal (2009): 29 of the 30 Sharpe ratios tested are within 0.03 of the published values, and all 26 turnover figures of the portfolios that trade are within 25 per cent. It then asks the paper's question under an index mandate on Ken French's 49 US industries. Applying the mandate's tilt without an optimiser realises 105.6 basis points a year of tracking error, 14.7 to 25.9 more than the optimised portfolios with the same tilt, each difference statistically significant at the 5 per cent level after Holm's correction. Page, dashboard, note, code

Which risk model for an index mandate? Covariance estimators and tracking error on 49 US industries. Runs a long-only index-tracking mandate on Ken French's 49 US industries from 1979 to 2026 with six covariance estimators. The factor and principal-component models realise 7 to 9 basis points a year more tracking error than the sample covariance's 84.7, each difference statistically significant at the 5 per cent level after Holm's correction. On minimum-variance portfolios, by which Dom, Howard, Jansen and Lohre (2025) judge estimators, the same two models realise 0.95 to 1.58 percentage points a year more volatility when weights may be negative, and within 0.06 points of the sample covariance when they may not. Page, dashboard, note, code

Climate risk as an equity signal. An ownership graph linking 328 ownership entities in the US and developed Europe, 302 of them listed companies with a price series, to 5,115 asset holdings, built from Global Energy Monitor's free tracker. Of 19 apparent ownership changes drawn at random and checked by hand, 12 had no corporate event behind them. Page, note, code

Stock-level index tracking: a point-in-time benchmark, a long-only mandate, and its performance attribution by sector. Rebuilds the S&P 500 month by month from the list of members kept by the Center for Research in Security Prices (CRSP), at a tracking error of 11.5 basis points a year from 1926 to 2024 against CRSP's own return of the same list, and runs a long-only index-tracking mandate on it from 1999 to 2024, with its active return attributed by sector. Under the four covariance estimators the mandate realises 78.9 to 83.6 basis points a year of tracking error, and each estimator forecasts less: the realised active return is 48 to 85 per cent more volatile than forecast. Index changes require 11.4 per cent of the turnover under the Ledoit-Wolf estimator. Page, dashboard, note, code

Return prediction: machine learning on the S&P 500's members, corrected for overfitting. Forecasts of each S&P 500 member's return over the next month from twelve characteristics built from CRSP and Compustat, by six models from least squares to a neural network, out of sample from 2006 to 2024. The study follows the method of Gu, Kelly and Xiu (2020), reduced to the index's members, and corrects the highest Sharpe ratio among the portfolios built from these forecasts for overfitting, that is, for having tried many versions and kept the one with the highest. In build: its findings are stated when the study is complete. Page, code
